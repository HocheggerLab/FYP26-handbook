    Annotation workshop · Solutions and discussion¶

    Open after thinking, discussing and recording your decisions. The worksheet contains the complete abstracts. Explanations below are teaching material, not required classifier output.

    Draft — supervisor sign-off required

    Agree uncertain conventions before the assigned fifty, especially the boundary between no and unsure and whether fusion names establish a terminus.

    The dashes mean omit these fields, not an additional label. Items 1, 2, 3 and 5 proceed to later full-text reading; item 4 is screened out, subject to a separate audit of rejected papers.

    1. A tag is clear; its locus is not¶

    The medaka spindle abstract says “combining CRISPR knock-in with auxin-inducible degron technology”. This supports AID tagging but does not identify the integration site or terminus. CRISPR alone does not establish native-locus tagging. Do not assume Ran is the tagged target simply because it is the subject of the biological finding.

    Discuss: what additional information would establish native-locus tagging?

    2. CRISPR disrupts the locus; a construct supplies the tagged protein¶

    The toolkit abstract describes “Sleeping Beauty transposon-based vectors for expressing auxin-inducible degron (AID)-tagged genes under the regulation of a tetracycline-controlled promoter”. The tagged protein comes from an ectopic construct: exogenous, not endogenous. Expression above native levels need not be demonstrated to use this label.

    Discuss: would this be useful precedent for a native-locus design, and which conclusions would need qualification?

    3. “Replaced” does not specify the method¶

    The EBF1 abstract says “we replaced EBF1 by EBF1-FKBP^F36V in pro-B cells, allowing the rapid degradation by adding the degradation TAG13 (dTAG13) dimerizer”. This identifies dTag, but “replaced” does not distinguish native-locus tagging from knockout plus rescue.

    The proposed conservative convention requires an explicit tag-position statement. A fusion name suggests an arrangement, but the later reference to the “C-terminal domain of EBF1” describes domain activity, not tag location. Agree the fusion-name convention before using it on the evaluation set.

    Discuss: unclear can be a confident classification of insufficient information.

    4. A natural degron is not an added tag¶

    The Myc abstract describes “a phosphorylated Myc degron centered at threonine 58 (T58)” and “a second Fbw7 phosphodegron at Myc T244”. These are natural sequence motifs, not added tags: no. Omit the other fields.

    This abstract screen does not establish that no tagged constructs appear anywhere in the full paper. That distinction is why rejected papers need an audit.

    Discuss: does uncertainty concern the described mechanism, or merely the possibility that unreported methods contain tags?

    5. Degradation chemistry and an unspecified construct¶

    The FEM1B abstract mentions “the development of a stable construct of FEM1B”. The PROTAC context does not establish tagging, but the unspecified construct makes unsure a proposed screening answer: read the full text later to resolve whether it is tagged. The remaining categories are unclear.

    The earlier excerpt-based example proposed no based on degradation chemistry alone. The complete abstract makes this a boundary case. If the supervisor chooses a convention that unspecified constructs are no, record it explicitly and apply it consistently; do not imply that no tag was used.

    Discuss: what is the cost of admitting this paper versus missing a tagging precedent?

    Debrief¶

    1. Identify one changed answer and the wording or rule responsible.
    2. Separate disagreements about the text from disagreements about conventions.
    3. Agree unresolved conventions with your supervisor.
    4. Distinguish unsure for screening from unclear for the other fields.

    Keep practice separate from the evaluation set, then begin Part 2.
