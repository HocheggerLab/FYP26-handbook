    Tagging and construction¶

    Enter two proteins, each with one combined mAID–SMASh tag. Both are degrons.

    Question Proposed answer Evidence location
    Protein Cyclin A2 (CCNA2) and cyclin B1 (CCNB1). First Results subsection; Fig. 1.
    Model Human hTERT RPE-1 cells: A2dd, B1dd and B1dd/B2ko. Cyclin B2 is knocked out in the last background, not degron-tagged. Fig. 1–2; cell-line-generation methods.
    Expression strategy Endogenous tagging of CCNA2 or CCNB1. Fig. 1B–C.
    Method Cas9/gRNA-assisted integration using donor homology arms; electroporation, antibiotic selection and clone screening. Methods, “Generation of endogenously tagged cell lines”.
    Position C-terminal for both cyclins. Fig. 1B.
    Architecture Cyclin–linker–mAID–SMASh, followed in the cassette by T2A–neomycin resistance. The linker DNA is reported in Methods. SMASh self-cleaves in the off condition; do not describe the entire cassette as a permanently intact protein fusion. Fig. 1A–B; cell-line-generation methods.
    Alleles Reported homozygous tagging. Do not infer complete copy-number or allele-by-allele sequence validation. B1dd/B2ko additionally has two reported CCNB2 frameshift alleles. Fig. 2A legend; cell-line-generation methods.
    Construct availability Described here: schematic, components, linker, gRNAs and PCR primers. Addgene 48139 is the Cas9 vector, not a verified deposit of the complete cyclin donor. Fig. 1B; cell-line-generation and “Genomic PCR” methods.
    Edit validation Genomic PCR and immunoblot screening; B2 knockout additionally checked by sequencing and protein loss. These validate editing, not normal function. Fig. 1C, Fig. 2A; methods.

    Function before depletion¶

    Proposed answer: inconclusive for equivalence to untagged protein.

    • Cyclin A2: untreated tagged cells provide cell-cycle and proliferation controls. A cycling cell line alone does not show that the tag leaves every cyclin A2 function intact. See Fig. 2B–E and Fig. 5A–C.
    • Cyclin B1: untreated B1dd/B2ko cells can divide successfully, supporting retained cellular function even without B2. See Fig. 3A–C and EV2A. This is not a demonstration of full equivalence to untagged B1.

    A narrowly qualified “retained in assays tested” for B1 may be defensible if the answer names successful division as the assay and acknowledges the comparison's limits. The supervisor should settle this convention before marking.

    Do not transfer the YFP rescue result to the mAID–SMASh fusion. They are different tagged versions.

    Depletion¶

    Question Proposed answer Evidence location
    Induction Doxycycline induces OsTIR1; IAA 500 μM activates the AID pathway; asunaprevir 3 μM inhibits SMASh cleavage. For immunoblotting, doxycycline is 1 μg/ml for 2 h before IAA/Asv. Together these are called DIA. Fig. 1A, F; “Immunoblotting” methods.
    Measurement Whole-cell immunoblots, ImageJ quantification and GAPDH normalisation, compared with control protein levels; three experiments for Fig. 1D–E. Fig. 1D–E; “Immunoblotting”.
    Efficiency Quantified: authors report approximately 50% depleted within 1 h and less than 5% remaining within 2–4 h. Do not invent separate exact endpoints from blot appearance. First Results subsection; Fig. 1D–E.
    Kinetics Time course above; combined activation is more effective than either system alone. This is not a fitted intrinsic half-life. Fig. 1D–F.
    Basal depletion Not reported in the inspected evidence as a dedicated basal-degradation measurement; provisional pending Appendix review. Untreated controls do not prove no leakiness. Main Results and Fig. 1–2.
    Recovery Not reported in the inspected evidence; provisional pending Appendix review. EdU washout is not DIA washout or cyclin recovery. “EdU pulse chase” methods; Fig. 5A–C.

    Keep protocol differences visible: widefield live-cell microscopy specifies 2 μg/ml doxycycline; Fig. 4A specifies 3 h before IAA/Asv addition. Do not apply the immunoblot protocol to every experiment.

    Function after depletion and specificity¶

    Protein/background Outcome and controls Evidence location
    A2dd Depletion reduces mitotic entry, increases the 4N population and prevents colony formation. G2-specific tracking supports a requirement for A2 in mitotic entry, not just an earlier replication defect. Fig. 2B–E; Fig. 4A–C.
    B1dd B1 depletion reduces but does not prevent colony formation; no discernible cell-cycle phase-distribution change is reported. Fig. 2B–D.
    B1dd/B2ko Depleting B1 when B2 is absent permits mitotic entry but causes segregation/cytokinesis failure and prevents proliferation. Fig. 2B–E; Fig. 3A–D.
    A2 specificity Reciprocal cyclin-abundance checks and siRNA provide support. Wee1/B55 interventions bypass the block; they are not same-protein A2 rescue. No degradation-resistant A2 rescue was identified in the inspected sources. Fig. 1G; Fig. 2H–K; Fig. 4–5.
    B1 specificity Cyclin B1–YFP and B1–YFP–NLS rescue segregation/cytokinesis defects; YFP alone does not. This supports the causal role of B-type cyclin loss and functionality of the rescue proteins. EV4A–C and accompanying Results.

    Take-home point¶

    Editing, depletion, baseline function and rescue answer different questions. A phenotype after degradation does not establish that the degron-tagged protein behaved normally beforehand.
