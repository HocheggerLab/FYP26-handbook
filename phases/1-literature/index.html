    Phase 1 · Literature¶

    Opens 27 October. Abstract classifications due 10 November. Three full-text reference papers ready for 17 November. Combined background/protein presentation after Christmas.

    Read about your protein alongside the evaluation work. Finish the evidence needed for the cloning design by 1 December; the written introduction is part of the combined introduction after Christmas. Follow the project schedule for Tuesday meetings and preparation.

    The seven parts¶

    Part numbers identify tasks, not seven consecutive weeks. In particular, Part 6's independent reference work starts on 10 November, so both abstract and full-text agents can be evaluated during 17–24 November.

    Part Task Output and timing
    1 · Your protein Read the supplied background literature Biological understanding now; combined background/protein presentation after Christmas, date, length and written format to be confirmed
    2 · Abstract classification Independently classify 50 abstracts Submitted, locked JSON before the 10 November meeting
    3 · Literature classification with an agent Prepare VS Code and uv before 3 November; later run the classifier on the same abstracts after reference locking Working setup first; saved predictions and run details, 17–24 November
    4 · Agent evaluation Compare with the independent reference; investigate disagreements and screening errors Results and limitations for review on 24 November
    5 · Gene-specific literature search Apply the reviewed screening workflow to your gene; check candidates and audit rejections Search record and candidate reading list, 24 November–1 December, subject to approval at the review
    6 · Full-text agent evaluation Independently extract three papers, then compare the extraction agent against the locked reference Human records 10–17 November; combined reference on 17 November; evaluation runs 17–24 November
    7 · Full-text tagging analysis Apply the evaluated workflow to relevant papers and verify its claims Tagging evidence, sources and uncertainties ready for cloning planning on 1 December

    Independent full-text reference · Three papers each¶

    Practise the recording format on separate examples on 3 November. On 10 November, your supervisor assigns three distinct papers per student, with no shared evaluation paper (15 papers across the group). Complete the independent extraction before the 17 November meeting. Record:

    • Protein and tag used.
    • Native-locus or ectopic expression strategy.
    • Tag position.
    • The experiment, if any, supporting retained protein function.
    • The location of supporting information in the paper or supplement.

    Do this without AI or access to the extraction agent's answers, even though you receive an agent account on 10 November. Keep these records separate from your locked abstract classifications.

    On 17 November, assemble the student and supervisor annotations. Preserve both originals, resolve disagreements in a separate reference and lock that reference before running the evaluations. This is a small pilot, not a comprehensive performance benchmark.

    From evaluation to your gene¶

    Review both agents on 24 November. If the workflow is suitable, use it to search and analyse your gene's literature during the following week. Save the responses and verify the claims that will inform your design. If it fails, agree corrections, a narrower scope or manual checking before proceeding.

    You also receive the isoform, modification and structure data on 24 November. Interpret these alongside the literature before the 1 December cloning planning session. These data are supplied; you do not need to build the underlying database yourself.

    Suitable evaluation papers can also contribute to the gene-specific analysis; do not repeat the extraction. If your gene has no suitable precedent, your supervisor supplies practice papers for Part 6. Part 7 has no fixed paper quota: analyse the relevant evidence found. “No precedent found” is not evidence that tagging will fail.

    How this informs the design¶

    Part 1 explains why the experiment is useful. Parts 2–7 establish what the literature workflow can do and what prior tagging experiments support. The fifty abstracts test a method; they need not concern your assigned gene. Why evaluate first?

    Literature, isoforms, modifications and structures together inform Phase 2 · Cloning. No single source guarantees that a tag will work. Bring the evidence and remaining uncertainties to the design session, rather than an unverified agent recommendation.
