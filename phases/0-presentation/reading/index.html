    Reading for the presentation¶

    Eight papers. That is the whole required list.

    A thousand words is short, and four weeks is not long. Eight papers read properly and cited accurately will produce a better introduction than twenty skimmed. If you want more, the shared Zotero library has it — but read these first, and only go further if something has caught you.

    Set Zotero up now, not in week 3

    Accept the invitation, install Zotero and the browser connector, and point your reference manager at the group. You need references in the written introduction, and sorting this out while you are also writing is a miserable way to spend the week of the deadline.

    The seven¶

    Degron tags — what you are putting on the protein

    Covers
    1 Nabet et al. 2018, Nat Chem Biol the dTAG system — what a degron tag is, and the figure showing a protein gone in 30 minutes
    2 Crncec & Hochegger 2022, Methods Mol Biol degron tagging as an actual protocol, in RPE-1 cells, from this lab

    Gene targeting — how it gets there

    Covers
    3 Doudna & Charpentier 2014, Science the CRISPR-Cas9 primer. One read gets you guides, PAMs and why a cut is only half the job
    4 Choi et al. 2023, PNAS tagging proteins at their own locus with HDR donors. This is roughly what you design in phase 2

    Agents, and whether anyone can tell if they are any good

    Covers
    5 Guo et al. 2026, Cell the overview: what "autonomous science" is being claimed, and where agents are actually being used in biology. Start the second half here
    6 Nair et al. 2026, bioRxiv CompBioBench — AI agents turned loose on computational biology and scored against real answers
    7 Kapoor et al. 2024, AI Agents That Matter why those scores are not to be trusted. The "agent benchmarks are broken" paper, and short
    8 Gu et al. 2024, BLADE ground truth built from several independent experts, because experts disagree. The closest published thing to your shared protein

    Papers 1–4 are the biology, and together they are one story: what the tag does, and how you get it onto the gene. Papers 5–8 are the measurement problem — 5 for what is being claimed, 6–8 for whether anyone can check it. Nothing here hands you the bridge between the two halves; that is the part that has to be yours.

    Read the figures before the text, especially in 1, 2 and 4.

    Two short explainers, before paper 5¶

    Neither is a paper and neither needs citing, but both are quicker than any of the above and will save you time.
    Anthropic, Building effective agents what a tool-calling loop is, written plainly
    Raschka, Components of a coding agent the same loop taken apart piece by piece

    One thing to notice as you read¶

    Paper 5 is in Cell. Papers 6, 7 and 8 are preprints — not peer reviewed. That gap is worth a sentence in your introduction: the overviews get refereed, while most of the work actually measuring these systems is still sitting on arXiv and bioRxiv, in a field moving faster than review can follow.

    The split you will have to pick a side on

    There are two ways to score an agent, and they disagree.

    Verifiable answer — ask something with one checkable answer and compare. Cheap, reproducible, and only works on narrow questions. That is paper 6.

    Trajectory and rubric — judge how the agent worked, not just what it concluded. Captures far more, but needs human judgement, which brings back exactly the annotator variance your ground truth is meant to pin down. That is paper 8.

    This project does both, which is why your answers get locked and why everyone annotates the shared protein. Noticing that tension is a very good thing to put in your introduction.

    If you want more¶

    The Zotero library is filed by topic — Degron tags, Agentic AI, Evaluating agents. Nothing in there is required for phase 0. Add anything good you find; it is a group library, so what you add everyone gets.

    Degraders, for contrast¶

    PROTACs and molecular glues destroy a protein without modifying the gene. Same outcome, completely different method. The difference is set out in Degrons and degraders — read that page rather than duplicating it in a talk. You do not have to cover degraders in your introduction.

    Where to search¶

    PubMed, and the papers that cite the eight above. A large part of phase 1 is discovering that keyword searching alone finds a surprisingly small fraction of the relevant literature — worth noticing early.

    Note that papers 6–8 are not in PubMed. Agent evaluation lives almost entirely on arXiv and bioRxiv, which is the first thing this project will teach you about searching a young field.
