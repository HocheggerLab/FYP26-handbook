    Phase 0 · Background¶

    Goal¶

    Draft the introduction to your thesis — and present it, in five minutes, on 3 November.

    You need to read about degron tags and about agentic AI in biology, and think about how agents could be used for degron tagging.

    What you hand in¶
    A written introduction about 1000 words, with references
    Five slides the same material, compressed to five minutes

    Both are due on 3 November. They are the same argument at two lengths, and writing the long one first makes the short one much easier.

    Time¶

    Weeks 1–4. Perhaps 30–40 hours in total, mostly reading.

    Before you start¶

    Read the four background pages in this section — what an AI agent is, evaluating agents, degrons and degraders and gene targeting. They are short and everyone reads them.

    You get two taught sessions before you present:

    When What
    Wk 1 Course overview — what the project is and why it is built this way
    Wk 3 Agentic AI and Claude Code, with a live agent run on screen
    3 Nov (wk 5) You present

    Steps¶

    1. Read both topics — degron tags, and agentic AI in biology. Everyone covers both; none of this is divided up between you.
    2. Read around them. Start from the reading list and the shared Zotero library; follow what looks interesting from there.
    3. Find a worked example for each half, and keep it concrete — an example beats a definition. For degron tags, take one from the literature: a real protein, really tagged, and the figure that proves the protein went away. For agents you will not find a tidy worked example in a paper, so use the week 3 demonstration — watch what the agent gets wrong and write it down — or the failure modes the benchmark papers report in aggregate.
    4. Write the introduction, about 1000 words with references.
    5. Cut it to five slides. Five minutes is roughly a slide a minute, and you will be stopped at five.
    6. Present on 3 November, to the group.

    What to cover¶

    Four things, in both the written intro and the talk. That is the exercise.

    Roughly
    1 What a degron tag is Why destroy a protein instead of deleting its gene — and why speed is the point
    2 What agentic AI in biology is The loop and the tools — not a chatbot — and what people are currently claiming it can do
    3 The problem of evaluation How do we know what counts as a good outcome in an agentic task?
    4 The bridge Could an agent design a degron tag, and how would we know if it did it well?

    Split the introduction roughly equally between the four. Point 4 is the one that requires your own thinking, so it is where the marks are most easily won or lost.

    Chemical degraders — PROTACs and molecular glues — are useful context and there is a page on them, but they are not something you have to cover.

    You keep both of these

    The 1000 words are the first draft of your thesis introduction, and the slides open your final presentation. You will revise both, but neither is thrown away — which is why they are worth doing properly now rather than the night before.

    Done when¶

    • The written introduction is handed in, with its references,
    • you have presented in five minutes, and
    • you can answer a question that was not on your slides.

    What happens next¶

    Your protein is assigned when phase 1 rolls out on 27 October — deliberately, so that this talk is about the method rather than about one gene. Phase 1 then runs alongside: your abstract classifications are due 10 November, and three independent full-text reference papers are prepared for 17 November. The written protein introduction is due after Christmas, on a date to be confirmed. See the project schedule.

    If you get stuck¶

    1. Re-read the background pages — they cover more of this than they look like they do.
    2. Bring a half-finished draft or slide deck to supervision. Much easier to fix than a blank one.
    3. Ask each other. You are all writing on the same topics.
    4. Then email.
