FYP26 — circadian clock and cancer: abstract classification

Classify from the supplied title and abstract only. Do not use AI or open the paper. Export after every session: browser storage is not a backup.

Instructions and allowed options
QuestionOptions
1 · CircadianThe abstract reports research on the circadian clock, circadian rhythms or circadian disruption (for example shift work or light at night).
2 · CancerThe abstract reports research on cancer or tumours.
3 · Breast cancerThe abstract reports research on breast cancer specifically.
4 · Clock geneThe abstract reports results on at least one named core clock gene or protein: BMAL1 (ARNTL), CLOCK, PER1–3, CRY1–2, NR1D1–2 (REV-ERB).
5 · EvidenceThe main kind of evidence reported.
6 · Expression dataThe abstract reports gene or protein expression measured in human tumour or patient samples.
7 · Full textTake forward: the abstract reports original results that link at least one named core clock gene or protein to cancer (tumour growth or cancer cell behaviour, expression in tumours, patient outcome, or treatment response).

Questions 1–4, 6 and 7: yes · no · unsure (the abstract does not settle it). Answer every question for every abstract, including the ones that are not about the clock or cancer.

Evidence: patient_samples_or_cohort (human tissue, tumours or patient cohorts) · cell_lines · animal_model · epidemiology (population studies, such as shift work and cancer risk) · review · not_stated. If several apply, choose the one the conclusions rest on.

Full text: yes and unsure both queue the paper for full-text reading. Reviews and commentaries are no; so is shift-work epidemiology that names no clock gene. Any cancer counts, not only breast.

An "epigenetic clock" or "mitotic clock" is not the circadian clock. Melatonin counts as circadian only when the abstract frames it as a rhythm or clock signal.
