    Timeline¶

    Eleven weeks this term, then the write-up in your own time over the vacation. We meet Tuesdays, 1–3pm, GDSC.

    Date Week What happens
    1–4 Phase 0 · Background — reading and your introduction
    27 Oct 4 Phase 1 rolls out. You get your 50 abstracts
    3 Nov 5 Presentation 1 — degron tags, agentic AI, and the bridge
    10 Nov 6 Abstract annotations due before the session. Working with agents — Claude accounts issued
    17 Nov 7 Protein intro due — slides and text. Code and skills for the literature analysis; quantifying starts
    24 Nov 8 Cloning and constructs, by hand
    8–11 Phase 2 · Cloning
    → Phase 3 · Analysis, into the vacation

    Week 1 is the week beginning 5 October 2026.

    The three currently dated hand-ins¶

    Due What
    1 3 Nov, presented Degron tags, agentic AI and the bridge — ~1000 words plus five slides. Phase 0
    2 10 Nov Your 50 annotated abstracts, as a JSON file. Part 2
    3 17 Nov Your protein introduction — one page and one or two slides, emailed. Part 1

    Only the first is presented. The other two are hand-ins.

    Why 10 November is the date that matters¶

    Your annotations are due the morning you get your Claude account.

    That is deliberate, and it is not a trust exercise. Those fifty judgements are what every model in this project is scored against, so they have to be yours — and they must be finished before you inspect model answers or the full papers. The no-AI rule also covers tools you already have.

    Preserve the independent reference

    Keep a local copy of your submitted JSON. Confirm receipt and locking before reading any of those full texts or inspecting model answers.

    Your supervisor annotates independently. Keep both original records and resolve disagreements in a separate reference before comparing models. Later corrections need a dated explanation, not a silent overwrite.

    Freezing a reference protects independence; it is not, by itself, a full pre-registration of the study. See Your Claude account.

    After that¶

    From 10 November you have an agent and are expected to use it — including to run the literature analysis over your own phosphatase, which is what the 17 November session sets you up for.

    Phase 1 roadmap after the abstract hand-in¶

    When / prerequisite Part Work
    10 Nov session onward, after abstract locking 3 · Literature classification with an agent Classify the same abstracts; save predictions and run details
    After Part 3; work begins with guidance during 10–17 Nov 4 · Agent evaluation Compare with the independent reference and investigate errors
    From 17 Nov, after Part 4 5 · Gene-specific literature search Search and screen your gene's literature; agree candidates and the evaluation papers
    After paper selection; deadline to be agreed 6 · Full-text agent evaluation Independently extract three papers per student; lock answers before running and comparing the extraction agent
    After Part 6; deadline to be agreed 7 · Full-text tagging analysis Apply the evaluated workflow to relevant papers; verify evidence for your design

    These are sequencing checkpoints, not additional fixed submission dates. Agree the Part 6 and 7 schedule alongside the cloning work at supervision. The full-text recording format will be supplied before Part 6 begins.

    Part 6 is a bounded pilot: three papers each, with a shared worked example first. Suitable gene-specific papers can also support Part 7 without repeating the extraction. Part 7 has no fixed paper quota. Keep the independent full-text reference separate from the already locked abstract answers.

    See the Phase 1 roadmap for outputs and scope.

    Phase 2 starts on 24 November, by hand. Dates for the phase 2 hand-in and the final presentation are confirmed at supervision.

    Over the vacation¶

    The analysis is yours to pace. You will have your model results, your annotations and a Claude subscription. What is left is the analysis and the writing.

    If you fall behind¶

    Tell us early. The work scales down cleanly: fewer abstracts, with the analysis unchanged.

    If you are short of time, agree a reduced scope with your supervisor rather than rushing: the later comparisons depend on careful reference answers.
