    How you are marked¶

    Read this in week 1, not week 11. Students calibrate effort to what they believe is assessed; if that is invisible you will guess, and usually guess wrong.

    This page is the policy — what is looked at, and what is not. The exact weights come from the FYP marking scheme and are confirmed at supervision.

    What is assessed everywhere¶

    Component What good looks like
    Rigour of the ground truth Consistent conventions, honest gaps, evidence that verifies, ambiguities logged as you met them
    Quantitative analysis Appropriate statistics, human agreement reported as context rather than an accuracy ceiling, uncertainty stated
    Interpretation You can say what each number means and what it does not
    Methods transparency Someone else could repeat this from your write-up
    Presentation and communication Thesis and talk are clear

    Ground-truth rigour carries the largest weight. It is the part nobody can do for you and the part everything downstream depends on.

    What is not assessed¶

    • Whether the models do well. Not your doing, and a model failing informatively makes a better thesis than one that quietly succeeds.
    • Volume. Careful and smaller beats rushed and larger. If the timeline slips we scale the work down, not the care.
    • Whether you wrote the analysis code yourself. See below.

    On using AI¶

    You have an account from 10 November, and using it is expected — for reading, for code, for drafting, for running your own agent over your gene.

    One exception: independent reference-building. Do not use AI to produce, summarise or check the human answers used to evaluate an agent. This applies to both abstract and full-text reference annotations, until they are locked. Those judgements are the measurement. Why.

    What is assessed is not whether you used AI. It is whether you can specify what you need, check that what came back is right, and interpret the result.

    Keep a log of what you asked for, what came back, and what you had to fix. Include it as an appendix. It is evidence of the skill being assessed, and usually the most honest thing in the thesis.

    When things are due¶

    See the timeline. Nothing is assessed before 3 November.
