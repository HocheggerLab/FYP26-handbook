    Your Claude account¶

    You each get a Claude subscription, paid for by the department through Claude for Science, and a proper introduction to Claude Code.

    You get it on 10 November, the morning your annotations are due. From then on you need it: the session that day sets you up with Claude Code, and the week after you use it to run the literature analysis over your own phosphatase.

    Where you must not use it¶

    Independent reference annotations used to evaluate an agent.

    This includes the fifty abstracts and any later manual full-text reference set. Do not use AI to summarise, interpret or check those items before the reference is locked. Also keep model answers out of human adjudication.

    Outside that reference-building work, use it for reading, searching, coding, drafting and running your agent. Follow the source restrictions for each task: abstract-only answers must be locked before you read the full papers.

    Why that one place¶

    You are building the measuring stick.

    The project rests on a single claim: that a careful person, working from the evidence, produces answers good enough to judge a machine against. Produce those answers with the machine and you have measured nothing — the model agrees with itself, you report 0.9, and the number means nothing at all.

    It is the same reason a scale is calibrated with a known weight rather than with whatever happens to be sitting on it.

    Plan several annotation sessions, not a single last-minute block. Time a few items after practice, then budget for the remainder, checking and export.

    The deadline comes before the account session to help keep this sequence clear. The restriction applies to all AI tools, including any you already have—not just the account we provide.

    What if I slip?

    Tell us, and say which items were affected. Nothing bad happens — we drop those items or mark them, and the dataset stays honest.

    Reporting this early lets us protect the comparison. Keep the original record and explain what happened; your supervisor will agree how to handle the affected items.

    What you get¶
    A Claude account Standard seat, yours for the project and the write-up
    Claude Code the agent, on your own machine, with a hands-on session
    A demonstration a real run on screen, including what goes wrong

    Keep a log¶

    From the moment you start using it: what you asked for, what came back, and what you had to fix.

    It goes in the thesis as an appendix, and it is usually the most honest thing in it. What is assessed is not whether you used AI — it is whether you can specify what you need, check that what came back is right, and interpret the result. The log is the evidence of that, and it is much easier to keep as you go than to reconstruct in week 11.

    See How you are marked.

    What the demonstration is for¶

    Later in the project you analyse what an agent actually did — how many steps it took, which tool calls failed, whether it noticed, whether it claimed to check something it never checked.

    You cannot analyse that having never watched it happen. So you see a real run, on a worked example, with the transcript on screen. Take notes: the things that go wrong are the interesting part, and they go wrong in ways you would not guess.
